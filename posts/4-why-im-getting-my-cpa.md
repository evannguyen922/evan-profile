## Why the CPA still matters

A CPA license is one of the most powerful things an accountant can earn. It lets you sign off on audit reports, and it shows employers and clients that you've mastered accounting, tax and ethics.

The [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/accountants-and-auditors.htm) backs this up. Public companies must have CPAs sign the reports they file with the SEC, and certification may improve job prospects. That's a strong reason to plan early.

## The 150-hour question

Most states used to require 150 credit hours plus one year of experience for a license. For most students, that meant an extra year of school.

Now a growing number of states, including Pennsylvania, also accept 120 credits, as long as you have two years of experience instead of one.

This matters for students like me. I want to start working right after graduation, and I'm only planning on a four-year degree because a master's is expensive. Check your state's current rules through [NASBA](https://nasba.org/) before you plan your schedule.

## Why I'm doing it anyway

Right now, I'm heading into Finance and EPM advisory work, where a CPA isn't required. But I've realized clients still trust consultants who understand the accounting behind the systems they're building.

My firm covers the cost of the exam and offers a bonus for passing, so I'm taking full advantage. I plan to start studying right after I graduate and pass most of my sections before my first day.
